## 1. Current Phase Position

WGV1.x produced the evidence material. It created optical thread evidence, prior SAR evidence inheritance, dual-evidence closure checks, azimuth-constrained temporal alignment checks, and GM_RM011 optical-behavior to SAR-GT-local mechanism readings.

WGV2.0-WGV2.3 converted that material into a permission-gated cross-modal mechanism factor graph route:

- WGV2.0 defined the cross-modal mechanism factor graph schema.
- WGV2.1 instantiated a bounded set of graph windows and proved weak or blocked items stayed weak or blocked.
- WGV2.2 propagated constraints and diagnosed missing factors.
- WGV2.3 defined a divergent idea-bank and strict admission boundary for future factors.

The project should no longer be framed as one-to-one optical-SAR mapping. The current object is cross-modal mechanism relation modeling.

The graph is for mechanism explanation, constraint propagation, contradiction diagnosis, and missing-factor prioritization. The graph is not a selector, not a scorer, not a final annotation engine, not a GT-revision tool, and not identity truth.

## 2. Why WGV2.3 Matters

WGV2.3 matters because it separates discovery from admission.

Missing-factor discovery should be divergent. It can collect ideas from optical behavior, SAR morphology, geometry, temporal continuity, imaging physics, failure cases, non-vehicle rejection, boundary/competitor evidence, permission analysis, and cross-scene comparison.

Factor admission should be strict. A factor is not admitted because it correlates with GT, helps a desired case, or stacks another weak cue onto a score. It must have runtime-safe observable evidence, physical interpretation, verifiable relation, counterexample conditions, evidence permission, and leakage-risk control.

GT is posthoc validation only. GT can validate or falsify a proposed mechanism, but cannot define factors, thresholds, candidates, scores, rankings, or runtime priors.

When GT is used, the method must explain both:

- why the mechanism fits the GT-local vehicle region;
- why it does not fit nearby non-target, outside-GT, background, road-edge, static-reflector, guardrail, or competing-object regions.

A GT-positive example without non-target negative contrast is not enough.

## 3. Repository Inheritance Principle

Future sessions must use the repository flexibly and historically:

- correct results are evidence;
- failed attempts are negative evidence;
- archived corrections are guardrails;
- old reports may contain reusable factors, source tables, visual vocabulary, and failure modes;
- outdated or wrong paths may still be useful if they explain what not to do;
- no prior result should be blindly trusted without checking scope, provenance, and whether it was later corrected.

Inherit the repository as a history of mechanism evolution, not as a flat set of approved facts.

Repository inheritance therefore means historical use with provenance. It does not mean all old outputs are current truth, and it does not mean failed reports should be ignored. A failed visualization, a paused support-wide metric, or an archived correction can be the best evidence for a future anti-pattern.

## 4. Known Mistakes To Avoid

Avoid these mistake families:

- Treating GT as runtime construction input.
- Using GT overlap as mechanism proof.
- Using GT-local morphology to define runtime thresholds.
- Treating SAR bright points as vehicles.
- Treating small support-internal components as baby cars.
- Treating support as a final box.
- Treating optical tracker ID, P4G ID, or thread ID as identity truth.
- Treating weak optical thread plus SAR posthoc overlap as upgraded identity.
- Relaxing preserved boundaries such as GM_RM011 M005/M016 without independent boundary evidence.
- Treating GM_RM011 as unusable simply because it was outside the clean paired pool.
- Treating GM_RM017 as clean proof merely because azimuth source or SAR overlap exists.
- Turning the factor graph into weighted heuristic fusion.
- Expanding tables without redefining the mechanism question.

These are forbidden because they collapse mechanism relation, evidence permission, and identity claims into one unsafe conclusion.

## 5. Reusable Old-Content Categories

### A. Correct Active Sources

Use these for current state and committed conclusions. Examples include WGV2.0-WGV2.3 reports, WGV2 CSV ledgers, and the current session-start document.

Required check: confirm the source is still active, committed, and not later corrected.

### B. Archived Correction Documents

Use archived corrections as guardrails and anti-pattern references. They explain why prior paths were corrected, paused, or forbidden.

Examples include physical-structure-first, GT support failure correction, GT-local energy-field atlas correction, and support-wide correction archives.

Required check: read them as boundary documents, not as permission to resume the old path.

### C. Failed Or Superseded Reports

Use failed attempts and superseded reports to identify forbidden shortcuts, missing factors, and negative examples. A failed atlas or over-broad support-wide proxy may be more useful as a warning than as a positive source.

Required check: identify the later correction and preserve the corrected interpretation.

### D. Visual Review Packs

Use visual review packs for optical behavior vocabulary, human-readable evidence, and review-target context. They are not final truth and cannot create identity proof.

Required check: keep manual or visual observations in review/posthoc status unless a later runtime-safe protocol redefines their use.

### E. SAR GT-Local Morphology Reports

Use SAR GT-local morphology reports for GT posthoc validation, vehicle-scale morphology vocabulary, positive GT-local fit, and negative contrast design. Do not use them for runtime construction.

Required check: separate SAR GT-local positive evidence from nearby non-target, outside-GT, background, road-edge, static-reflector, guardrail, and competing-object comparisons.

### F. Support-Failure Audits

Use support-failure audits for missing-factor diagnosis and support construction feedback. They can show truncation, range/azimuth misalignment, temporal compensation gaps, neighbor contamination, too-broad support, or too-narrow support.

Required check: support is a feasible hypothesis field, not a final box.

### G. Dropout / No-Match / Blocked Pools

Use dropout, no-match, and blocked pools for mechanism failure explanation. Do not use them as clean paired validation.

Required check: preserve the pool semantics: 215 paired rows, 195 GM_RM011 blocked_missing_object_stream rows, 20 SAR-only rows, and 12 dropout/no-match temporal-continuation rows remain distinct.
